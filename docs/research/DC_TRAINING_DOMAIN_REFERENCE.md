# DC Training Domain Reference

Canonical research reference: historical Doggcrapp / DC Training doctrine only. No modern reinterpretation, application-design decisions, implementation guidance, schemas, or plans.

## Source classifications

- **[Direct Dante]** — a verified forum post authored by Dante Trudel.
- **[Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle]** — Dante Trudel's direct interview answer, reproduced on IntenseMuscle; not Dante forum authorship.
- **[Official DC/IntenseMuscle]** — The DOGG Pound sticky, article/editorial material, or established forum guidance not directly authored by Dante.
- **[Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante]** — a quotation attributed to Dante and preserved by a moderator or member; the original Dante post was not independently located.
- **[Secondary interpretation]** — an unofficial compiled manual or guide. Used only to expose gaps, examples, or conflicts.

IntenseMuscle identifies The DOGG Pound as DC's official home. Its archive says no official Dante-written manual exists; circulating manuals compile older posts. Direct posts and direct interview answers therefore control when sources differ. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

## 1. Core principles

- DC is an advanced bodybuilding system, not a beginner routine. Dante explicitly preferred that beginners use another program. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/30800-dc-for-advanced-beginners)

- Its training core is heavy progressive loading, very low work-set volume, higher body-part frequency, multi-rep rest-pause, extreme stretching, logbook accountability, and blast/cruise periods. Dante's broader system also included recovery, nutrition, and cardio practices. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

- "Heavy" means progressively heavy within the applicable rep range—not maximum-weight singles or sacrificing form. Reps use an explosive positive and controlled negative; Dante did not prescribe one universal eccentric duration. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

- Central objective: gain rep-strength on productive exercises over time. Rest-pause, rotation, and frequency serve that objective; novelty and soreness are not progress measures. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

## 2. Workout structure

- **A:** chest → shoulders → triceps → back width → back thickness.
- **B:** biceps → forearms → calves → hamstrings → quadriceps.

This order leaves the most systemically demanding work—back thickness and quads—last. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

Classic schedule:

| Week | Monday | Wednesday | Friday |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A1 | B1 | A2 |
| 2 | B2 | A3 | B3 |
| 3 | Repeat A1 | Repeat B1 | Continue |

- Body parts are trained three times per 14 days—roughly twice every eight days.
- Each specific exercise returns once every 14 days.
- One exercise is performed for each body-part category during a workout. Back width and back thickness are separate categories, not two exercises for one generic "back" slot.

[Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Official DC/IntenseMuscle cycle chart](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

Dante also used a three-way split for exceptionally advanced and strong trainees. A moderator-preserved quotation attributes to Dante the view that the classic two-way schedule generally produced faster overall gains because of its higher frequency. The three-way split is a specialized variant, not the default DC rotation. [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/5315-some-of-my-favorite-quotes-by-doggcrapp/page8)

## 3. Exercise rotation

- Each body part has three exercises. A1/B1 use the first choices, A2/B2 the second, and A3/B3 the third. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

- Exercises remain fixed while progression continues. Rotation is scheduled recovery and progression—not random workout-time variety. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

- No immutable "approved exercise" list exists in the direct material reviewed. Published pools are examples. Governing test: can the trainee progress on the movement over time while performing it safely? [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

- Large compound movements are usually favored because they offer more loading runway. Isolation exercises are not categorically forbidden; Dante contrasted limited-progression movements such as kickbacks with movements such as close-grip presses. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

- Free weights are preferred when they can be taken safely to failure. Smith and Hammer Strength machines are valid—especially for solo trainees who must safely rack a load at each failure point. Machine use is a safety tool, not an ideological rule. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13488-smith-machine-article)

## 4. Set/protocol types by body part

The table records published examples and usual recommendations, not universal DC prescriptions. Dante explicitly varied ranges and set structures by trainee, exercise, joints, recovery, age, and safety; an individualized prescription must not be turned into a rule for everyone. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Official DC/IntenseMuscle cycle chart](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp)

| Body part | Published classic handling |
|---|---|
| Chest | One rest-pause work set; commonly 11–15 total reps |
| Shoulders | One rest-pause work set; commonly 11–20 |
| Triceps | Presses commonly 11–20 RP; extensions commonly 15–30 RP for elbow safety |
| Back width | One rest-pause work set; commonly 11–15 or 11–20 |
| Back thickness | Large thickness movements generally use straight sets. A common interview template uses two deadlift/rack-deadlift sets around 6–8 and 10–12; rows are often one straight set around 10–12. Exact structure varies. |
| Biceps | One rest-pause work set; commonly 11–20 |
| Forearms | One straight set; commonly 10–20 |
| Calves | One specialized straight set; classically about 10–12 |
| Hamstrings | Leg curls commonly 15–30 RP; sumo leg press commonly 15–25 straight |
| Quadriceps | Usually a heavy straight set, often 4–8, followed by a lighter-but-heavy 20-rep widowmaker; not an absolute prescription for every trainee |

### Back thickness

Large, fatigue-sensitive back-thickness movements generally are not rest-paused because fatigue plus large loads can degrade form and create unacceptable risk. Exact set count, ordering, and rep ranges vary by movement and trainee. The often-cited 6–8 followed by a lighter 10–12 is one common interview template, not a universal rule; direct material also records other arrangements. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp)

### Hamstrings

Protocol follows the exercise:

- Leg curls: commonly rest-pause.
- Sumo leg presses: commonly a straight set.
- Stiff-legged deadlifts: no single canonical treatment. Documented methods include one straight set around 10–15, two straight ranges, progressively heavier sets of six, and—in selected cases—higher-rep rest-pause. Dante explicitly warned that individual trainee prescriptions were being incorrectly turned into universal rules.

[Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp), [Official DC/IntenseMuscle discussion](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/35543-stiff-leg-deadlifts)

### Quads

Usual classic recommendation after ramping warm-ups:

1. One brutally heavy straight set, often about 4–8 reps.
2. Rest several minutes.
3. Reduce weight but keep it heavy; perform one 20-rep widowmaker.

This is usual classic handling, not an absolute prescription for every exercise or trainee. Dante documented individualized departures where the usual widowmaker arrangement was unsuitable. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Direct Dante individualized example](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/32722-widowmaker-for-quads)

A widowmaker is one extended straight set—not three rest-pause mini-sets. After continuous reps become impossible, breathing pauses may occur while safely supporting the load, followed by singles or short clusters until 20. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/6796-widow-maker-set?p=171803)

An unofficial historical guide describes using a different widowmaker movement when the heavy movement is unsuitable for high reps—for example, a heavy free squat followed by a hack squat or leg press. This is secondary interpretation, not a universal direct-Dante rule. The direct individualized example establishes variability, not a general requirement to use two movements. [Secondary interpretation](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18856-dogg-crapp-411), [Direct Dante individualized example](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/32722-widowmaker-for-quads)

### Calves

Classic historical repetition:

1. Rise strongly onto the big toe.
2. Lower for about five seconds.
3. Reach full stretch.
4. Remain stretched for roughly 10–15 seconds.
5. Repeat for about 10–12 straight-set reps.

The bottom hold is part of every repetition; this is why calves traditionally receive no separate extreme stretch. The detailed movement description survives as a member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante, while the broader classic protocol also appears in a direct interview. [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/9874-extreme-stretches-courtesy-of-the-inhuman-one), [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

Dante later used selected-trainee variants with shorter two-to-five-second bottom holds and rest-pause totals around 20–30. Those were individualized tweaks, not a wholesale replacement of the classic protocol. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/43474-new-calve-protocol)

## 5. Rest-pause rules

One DC rest-pause work set ordinarily contains:

1. Normal repetitions to failure.
2. Rack or release the load; take approximately 10–15 deep breaths.
3. Second mini-set to failure.
4. Another 10–15 deep breaths.
5. Third mini-set to failure.

Thus: three failure points, two breathing breaks, one total work set. A classic example is roughly 8 + 4 + 2 = 14 RP. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

Additional rules:

- Breath count governs recovery; approximately 20–25 seconds is descriptive, not a universal stopwatch prescription.
- Trainee releases or safely racks the load during breaks.
- Failure means inability to complete another normal repetition with acceptable execution—not permission to deform technique.
- Positive is forceful; negative remains controlled.
- Traditional logbook notation treats the mini-sets as one total RP performance, such as `225 × 15 RP`. The breakdown explains the performance, but the total is the principal comparison.

[Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

Rest-pause is mainly used for chest, shoulders, triceps, biceps, back width, and suitable hamstring movements. It is normally excluded from quads, unsupported or high-load back-thickness compounds, forearms, and the classic calf protocol. Exercise stability and the trainee's circumstances can change the prescription. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp)

## 6. Logbook/progression rules

"Beat the logbook" means improve the last performance on that exact exercise:

- Same weight, more reps; or
- More weight while remaining within that exercise's applicable target range.

Dante emphasized weight progression inside the range. Example principle: moving from 225 × 15 RP to 230 × 11 RP remains a win when both results fit an 11–15 range. A lower rep total after adding weight is therefore not automatically a loss. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

Consequences:

- A rep range is evaluated as a range, not one mandatory number.
- Progression is not "add weight every workout regardless of result."
- Form must remain comparable; distorted execution does not constitute progression.
- Because each exercise returns about every two weeks in the classic schedule, comparison is against that exercise's previous appearance—not the immediately preceding workout.

[Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it), [Official DC/IntenseMuscle cycle chart](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

Straight-set exceptions still use load-and-rep progression, but the reviewed historical sources do not define one universal combined win score for multi-set protocols. Heavy quad set, widowmaker, and multiple back-thickness sets remain distinct performances.

## 7. Exercise replacement rules

- Continue an exercise while meaningful progression continues.
- One failed attempt to beat the prior entry can receive a "mulligan" when illness, stress, or an off day plausibly explains it.
- **If the trainee fails to beat the previous logged performance on that same exercise on two consecutive appearances, the normal DC response is to replace or rotate out that exercise, subject to the documented mulligan/off-day nuance.** [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

Dante rejected spending months extracting occasional token reps from a true plateau. Replacement begins a new progression run on another safe, productive exercise. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

An old exercise may return later. Its prior record remains historically meaningful; replacement is not deletion or a judgment that the exercise is permanently unusable. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

A direct Flex interview gives a simplified one-miss formulation. The more specific direct forum clarification supplies the one-mulligan and two-consecutive-appearances nuance used above. The source tension remains recorded rather than silently reconciled. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it)

## 8. Blast/cruise

### Blast

A blast is the all-out progression period: fixed rotation, failure-based work sets, and sustained logbook pressure.

- An early interview gives an approximate 6–12-week range. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)
- A later Dante post says most trainees land around 7–9 weeks. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/27568-how-long-is-your-blasts-cruises)

Practical end triggers include systemic fatigue, declining enthusiasm, recovery problems, or widespread slowing—not one isolated exercise plateau. This trigger wording is established forum practice rather than a rigid Dante formula. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/15617-dante-what-is-a-good-time-to-cruise)

### Cruise

Cruise is mandatory recovery from all-out training.

- About 7–14 days is common; Dante identified about 10 days as typical.
- Earlier interview material allowed roughly 10–14 and occasionally 21 days.
- Complete time away from weights and lighter maintenance training were both accepted.
- Non-negotiable principle: do not continue lifting hard.

[Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/27568-how-long-is-your-blasts-cruises), [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted)

No single universal cruise workout exists. IntenseMuscle practice commonly removes rest-pause, widowmakers, statics, and extreme stretching while using lighter straight sets, but that is community guidance—not one mandatory Dante protocol. Full time off or light maintenance can both satisfy the recovery purpose; hard lifting does not. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/18323-question-about-cruising), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/27568-how-long-is-your-blasts-cruises)

### Starting a new blast: statement versus inference

**Explicit attributed statement:** A moderator-preserved quotation attributed to Dante says not to demand immediate repetition of the previous blast's ending numbers. It explicitly says the first six workouts establish fresh baselines; those baselines may be above or below the prior blast's ending numbers; progression should move beyond prior peaks by the new blast's end. The same preserved quotation says to ease extreme stretching back in during the first week. The original Dante post containing this wording was not located. [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/5315-some-of-my-favorite-quotes-by-doggcrapp?p=179461)

**Reasonable inference under the classic two-way rotation:** Six consecutive workouts map to `A1 → B1 → A2 → B2 → A3 → B3`, so each rotated exercise appears once and receives a new-blast baseline. The quoted statement itself does not name these slots or explicitly say "one complete rotation." That equivalence comes from applying the official classic cycle chart to the explicit six-workout statement. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle cycle chart](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

**Forum interpretation / logical implication:** Prior-blast records remain historical context, while early new-blast performances become the current comparison baselines. The source explicitly says prior ending numbers are not mandatory immediate targets; it does not explicitly prescribe a record-retention model. History continuing while the immediate baseline resets is therefore interpretation, not a separately proven Dante rule.

## 9. Warm-ups

- DC has no mandatory warm-up count or universal warm-up sequence.
- Perform as many progressive sets as personally needed—often one to five—to prepare for the all-out work set.
- Stronger trainees handling larger loads generally require more ramps.
- Warm-ups prepare; they should not become competing work sets.

[Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

Dante's interview says logbook weights and reps exclude warm-ups. Community members sometimes recorded warm-ups voluntarily, but that is not Dante's stated logging method; warm-ups were not performances to beat. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Official DC/IntenseMuscle community example](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/26551-dc-training-log-spreadsheet-for-your-use)

## 10. Extreme stretches

Extreme stretching is part of classic DC, not an unrelated accessory.

- It is performed after the relevant body part has been trained.
- It may occur immediately after that body part or after a related block when equipment logistics require it.
- Historical material applies it to chest, shoulders, triceps, biceps, back, hamstrings, and quads.
- Calves normally need no separate stretch because their classic work-set protocol embeds one.

[Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18116-still-have-a-few-questions-about-dc-training-after-reading-about-it), [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/9874-extreme-stretches-courtesy-of-the-inhuman-one)

No universal timer applies. Many preserved examples use about 45–60 seconds; later direct safety material discusses roughly 60–90 seconds, and movement-specific exceptions exist. Stretch quality, control, and safety govern rather than one duration for every movement and trainee. [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/9874-extreme-stretches-courtesy-of-the-inhuman-one), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/31895-ok-im-going-to-bitch-a-little-bit-here-about-extreme-stretching)

Common historical forms preserved in the official stretch sticky include:

- Chest: loaded fly-position stretch.
- Shoulders: behind-body bar stretch.
- Triceps: overhead dumbbell stretch.
- Biceps: behind-body bar stretch.
- Back: weighted hang or rounded-back pull.
- Hamstrings: elevated straight-leg stretch.
- Quads: supported sissy-squat-style lean.

These detailed movement descriptions are member-preserved quotations attributed to Dante, not independently located original Dante posts. [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/9874-extreme-stretches-courtesy-of-the-inhuman-one)

Dante's stated purpose included recovery, fascia expansion, and possible hyperplasia. He described hyperplasia as theoretical; these are historical DC claims, not established here as scientific fact. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

Safety boundary: a controlled muscular stretch may be painful, but joint pain, unstable positions, or turning the stretch into a maximal lift are not intended. Use less load or another stretch when a version feels unsafe. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/31895-ok-im-going-to-bitch-a-little-bit-here-about-extreme-stretching)

## 11. Anything else the app would need to understand

Domain facts only—not application recommendations:

- **Abs sit outside the classic ten-part rotation.** The appended editorial material in the Flex interview article says they may be trained on any day, typically with one warm-up and one working set each of a crunch and leg-raise movement. It lists 20–30 RP or 15–20 straight as suggested work ranges. This is official article/editorial material, not a verified direct Dante quotation. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

- **Protocol belongs partly to the exercise, not only the body part.** A leg curl and stiff-legged deadlift are both hamstring exercises but may use completely different set structures. A stabilized row might sometimes be rest-paused while an unsupported row should remain straight. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp)

- **One "set" is not one universal performance shape.** DC contains RP totals, ordinary straight sets, variable back-thickness structures, heavy-plus-widowmaker quad work, and tempo-plus-hold calf repetitions. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp)

- **Static holds appeared in older DC examples but are secondary and optional.** Forum guidance describes them as a small addition after the RP set, not a defining requirement. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/17886-starting-static-hold)

- **Safety changes the prescription.** Exercise stability, solo versus spotted lifting, joint condition, age, and ability to preserve form can change exercise choice, target range, or whether RP is permitted. Age-related range guidance is general safety guidance rather than a hard universal cutoff. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/27456-for-every-bodybuilder-post-34-years-of-age/page6)

## 12. Uncertainties or conflicting source material

1. **Rep ranges are examples and individualized guidance, not universal defaults.** Classic charts contain many 11–15 RP ranges. Dante later gave general safety guidance for advanced trainees after roughly age 34: approximately 15–30 RP for many compound movements and 20–30 for lever or extension movements, with possible lower-rep deadlift exceptions. This is not a hard age cutoff or a silent replacement of every historical chart. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/27456-for-every-bodybuilder-post-34-years-of-age/page6)

2. **Back-thickness structure varies.** One common interview template presents a heavy set around 6–8 followed by a lighter set around 10–12. Earlier direct material also records 6–8 followed by a heavier 3–4 set and other individualized arrangements. No single set count, order, or range covers every movement or trainee. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp)

3. **Stiff-legged deadlifts have no one settled protocol.** Straight 10–15, two straight ranges, progressive sixes, and selected higher-rep RP all appear in established material. Dante says individual prescriptions should not become universal doctrine. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp), [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/35543-stiff-leg-deadlifts)

4. **Quad handling is usual, not absolute.** Exact heavy-set ranges vary by source and exercise: examples include 4–8, 4–6 for squats, and 6–10 for presses or hacks. Heavy set plus 20-rep widowmaker is the usual classic recommendation, but Dante documented individualized departures. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2), [Direct Dante individualized example](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/32722-widowmaker-for-quads)

5. **Widowmaker movement may differ from the heavy movement, but source strength is limited.** Same-movement handling is common. The differing-movement example is preserved in an unofficial guide and is therefore secondary. Dante's direct individualized leg-press instruction proves variability, not a universal two-movement rule. [Secondary interpretation](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18856-dogg-crapp-411), [Direct Dante individualized example](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/32722-widowmaker-for-quads)

6. **Calf protocol evolved.** The classic version is one straight 10–12 set with about a five-second negative and 10–15-second bottom stretch. Dante later used selected-trainee variants with two-to-five-second bottom holds and RP totals around 20–30. Later tweaks did not erase the classic protocol. [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/9874-extreme-stretches-courtesy-of-the-inhuman-one), [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/43474-new-calve-protocol)

7. **Cruise training is intentionally non-rigid.** Dante required recovery and no hard lifting but allowed complete time off or light maintenance work. Specific lighter straight-set cruise templates are established forum practice, not one canonical prescription. [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/27568-how-long-is-your-blasts-cruises), [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/pound-puppy-forum/18323-question-about-cruising)

8. **Statics are historically present but not core doctrine.** Older examples include them; later official guidance repeatedly tells new trainees to omit them and focus on progression. [Official DC/IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/17886-starting-static-hold)

9. **No closed approved-exercise canon exists in the direct material reviewed.** Published lists are useful examples. Governing principle remains safe, sustained progression. Claims that one machine, free-weight category, or specific exercise is universally required or forbidden exceed the reviewed direct statements. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13488-smith-machine-article)

10. **Extreme-stretch duration and mechanism are not universal or settled.** Preserved examples often use 45–60 seconds; later direct safety material uses 60–90, with movement-specific exceptions. Dante called potential hyperplasia theoretical. [Moderator/member-preserved quotation attributed to Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/9874-extreme-stretches-courtesy-of-the-inhuman-one), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/31895-ok-im-going-to-bitch-a-little-bit-here-about-extreme-stretching), [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/20286-updated-dc-training-newbies-read-this-first-and-then-ask-questions-later/page2)

11. **Failure and safety create a real boundary.** DC demands genuine failure on RP mini-sets yet rejects movements or loading situations where fatigue destroys form. Historical material does not provide one mechanical failure detector; safe execution remains exercise- and trainee-dependent. [Direct Dante interview reproduced on IntenseMuscle](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/16383-dogg-crapp-training-manual-latest-version-wanted), [Direct Dante](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/13472-straight-sets-vs-rp)

12. **Compiled manuals cannot resolve conflicts by authority.** The prominent `Dogg Crapp 411` guide labels itself unofficial. It is useful historical organization, but direct Dante posts and interviews take precedence when wording differs. [Secondary interpretation](https://www.intensemuscle.com/forum/main-forums/the-dogg-pound/18856-dogg-crapp-411)

13. **The original source for the "first six workouts" wording was not located.** Available evidence is a moderator's quotation compilation attributed to Dante. The statement explicitly says six workouts; it does not explicitly say those workouts are one complete A1/B1/A2/B2/A3/B3 cycle. That mapping is a reasonable inference only under the classic two-way schedule. The view that old records remain while new-blast performances become current baselines is forum interpretation / logical implication, not an explicit record-retention rule.
